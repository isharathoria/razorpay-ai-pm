Internal knowledge · RAG agent

A RAG agent to accelerate healthcare-system onboarding

Turning fragmented product, technical and deployment knowledge into an assistant that gives grounded answers, so new team members stop waiting on experts.

RAGRetrieval designPromptingEvals
10 → 6 months
Onboarding timeEnd-to-end onboarding time for a new US healthcare system, about 40% faster.

Problem

Onboarding a new US healthcare system meant navigating scattered product, technical, integration, deployment and process documentation. New team members depended on experienced colleagues for answers, the same questions kept repeating, and onboarding slowed down.

Why RAG, not a generic LLM

The answers were company-specific, current and contextual, which a general model doesn't know. Retrieval keeps every answer grounded in approved internal sources and traceable back to them, and the knowledge base can be updated without retraining.

Clinical workflow · LLM-powered tool

An LLM-powered clinical workflow that clinicians can trust

Designing an AI-assisted clinical decision-support workflow from first principles: the prompts, the evaluation criteria and the clinician interactions. There was no existing playbook, and trust and safety really mattered.

LLM workflowsPrompt engineeringEvaluation designHuman-in-the-loop
40% → 70%
Follow-up rateShare of cases that received follow-up after the LLM-powered workflow went live.

Problem

An LLM is only useful in a clinical setting if its output is reliable, easy to check, and fits how clinicians already work. A capable model was not enough. The workflow around it had to make the output trustworthy and usable.

The PM challenge

Define what "good" looks like before shipping: what the model should do, how to tell when its output is right, and where the clinician stays in control. Then build the prompts, checks and interactions around those answers.

What I owned as PM
Framed the use caseDecided where AI assistance adds value in the clinical workflow, and where it shouldn't.
Designed the promptsUsed prompt engineering to make outputs consistent and scoped to the task.
Defined evaluationSet the criteria for a "good" output and the checks before it reaches a clinician.
Designed the interactionShaped how clinicians see, verify and act on AI output, with a human in the loop.
Tested assumptionsSeparated what was true from what we had assumed, using real cases.
Balanced trust & speedWeighed usefulness against safety in a regulated domain.

Go-to-market · Automation

Automating product demos and sales decks

I took a manual, repetitive job and automated it: building tailored product demos and decks for each prospect. AI now generates them from product knowledge and customer context, ready for a quick review.

Workflow automationLLM generationTemplatesGTM enablement
↓ Product bandwidth
Product team time freedThe product team no longer has to build each new product demo and deck by hand.

Problem

Every customer conversation needed a demo and deck tailored to that health system's use case. Building them by hand was slow and repetitive, and quality and messaging varied depending on who made them.

The opportunity

Most of each deck was the same: product facts, the value story, the demo flow. What changed was the customer context. AI could combine the two on demand, so people could spend their time on the conversation instead of the slides.

What I owned as PM
Spotted the wasteNoticed the repetitive manual prep behind every demo and deck.
Separated fixed from variableSplit each deck into reusable product content and the parts tailored to each customer.
Built the automationSet up an AI workflow that generates the demo script and deck from those inputs.
Kept quality highBuilt in templates and a human review step so the output stays on-brand and accurate.
Drove adoptionMade it the default way the team prepared for customer conversations.
Made it reusableDesigned it so new products and use cases slot into the same pipeline.
